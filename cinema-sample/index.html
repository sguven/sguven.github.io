Cinema Bulletin · November 4–11, 2026

Prepared Wednesday, November 4, 2026 · Showtimes can change; confirm before you go

This week's 3 picks: Martin McDonagh's Wild Horse Nine and Lance Hammer's Queen at Sea at Angelika Houston, and Club Kid on 35mm at Village East.

Opening this week

Wild Horse Nine MUST-SEE

Searchlight Pictures · dark comedy, thriller · 118 min · R

Nov 6 (first shows Thu Nov 5) · ANGELIKA HOUSTON · Thu 2:00, 4:45, 5:45, 7:30, 8:30 pm · Fri 10 shows, 10:00 am–10:05 pm

Story: Shortly before the 1973 coup in Chile, the CIA sends two long-time partners (John Malkovich, Sam Rockwell) from Santiago to Easter Island, where a bond with two university students complicates things. Written and directed by Martin McDonagh; Steve Buscemi, Tom Waits and Parker Posey co-star.

Why see it: McDonagh's first film since The Banshees of Inisherin; Malkovich won Venice's Volpi Cup for Best Actor for the role. There is a lot to talk about in how it mixes a historic political rupture with black comedy.

Reaction: RT 94% (129 reviews), Metacritic 80; Venice was split, with some critics feeling McDonagh repeats familiar tricks.

Queen at Sea MUST-SEE

Greenwich Entertainment · drama · 121 min · not rated

Since Oct 30 · ANGELIKA HOUSTON · Wed 10:10 am, 1:00, 3:50, 6:40, 9:30 pm · Thu 10:05 am, 12:15, 3:00 pm

Story: University lecturer Amanda (Juliette Binoche) cares for her mother Leslie (Anna Calder-Marshall), who has advanced dementia; when a question of consent arises between her mother and her stepfather Martin (Tom Courtenay), the family faces a painful moral dilemma. Written, directed and edited by Lance Hammer.

Why see it: Hammer's second feature, 18 years after Ballast (2008); it won the Silver Bear Jury Prize in Berlin, and Courtenay and Calder-Marshall shared the Silver Bear for supporting performance. A quiet, observant actors' film that deserves a dark theater and full attention.

Reaction: RT 95% (20 reviews); Peter Bradshaw gave it five stars in the Guardian. Critics also stress that it is very sad and hard to watch.

Club Kid MUST-SEE

A24 · comedy-drama · 122 min · rating not confirmed

Nov 6, limited release · VILLAGE EAST, 35mm · Thu Nov 5 4:15, 7:00, 9:45 pm · Fri 11:20 am, 2:00, 4:45, 10:40 pm · daily shows through Nov 11

Story: Peter, a party promoter hanging on in New York's underground nightlife, suddenly has to take responsibility for a 10-year-old son he never knew he had. Jordan Firstman stars in his first feature as writer-director; Cara Delevingne and Diego Calva co-star.

Why see it: Premiered to a standing ovation in Cannes' Un Certain Regard, and A24 bought it for $17 million. Seeing a film about New York's queer nightlife on 35mm at Village East is an experience of its own.

Reaction: RT 100%, Metacritic 85; critics, including Sight and Sound, find it both very funny and moving.

Godzilla Minus Zero RECOMMENDED

GKIDS · kaiju, action · 2 h 15 min · R

Nov 6 · wide release · IMAX (not at Angelika)

Story: In 1949, two years after Godzilla Minus One, the Shikishima family (Ryunosuke Kamiki, Minami Hamabe) faces a new disaster. Takashi Yamazaki wrote, directed and supervised the visual effects.

Why see it: The first Japanese production shot for IMAX; a film for the biggest screen, not for home. Check whether the showing is subtitled or dubbed when you book.

Reaction: RT 93% (57 reviews); early reviews call the sequel bigger and louder, with emotional power close to the first film.

I Play Rocky RECOMMENDED

Amazon MGM Studios · biopic, drama · 2 h 8 min · PG-13

Nov 6 · limited release (NYC theaters not confirmed), expected to expand later this month

Story: An unknown young Sylvester Stallone (Anthony Ippolito) refuses to give up, despite every rejection, on starring in the Rocky script he wrote. Directed by Peter Farrelly; Matt Dillon and AnnaSophia Robb co-star.

Why see it: A making-of story from the New Hollywood era; it pairs well with the Rocky 50th-anniversary 4K screenings on November 7–11.

Reaction: RT 87% (62 reviews); it won over audiences at TIFF, though some critics, like the Daily Beast, felt it stays in the original's shadow.

Mouse RECOMMENDED

Independent Film Company / Sapan Studio · coming-of-age drama · 120 min · rating not confirmed

Nov 6 · limited release: IFC Center · expands Nov 13

Story: Just before senior year, Callie and Minnie's friendship falls apart, and Minnie grows close to Callie's mother Helen (Sophie Okonedo). Directed by Kelly O'Sullivan and Alex Thompson; David Hyde Pierce and Iman Vellani co-star.

Why see it: Premiered in the Berlinale Panorama; Okonedo's performance stands out. A small, well-written character film, even if it is not at Angelika.

Reaction: RT 100% (19 reviews); RogerEbert.com gave it four out of four stars.

Everest: The Other Side RECOMMENDED

Walt Disney / National Geographic · documentary · 1 h 50 min · PG-13

Since Oct 30 · ANGELIKA HOUSTON · Wed 11:10 am, 1:50, 4:25, 9:50 pm · Thu 10:45 am, 12:50, 3:25, 10:10 pm

Story: Ski mountaineers Hilaree Nelson and Jim Morrison plan to ski straight down Everest's north face. Directed by Jimmy Chin and Elizabeth Chai Vasarhelyi.

Why see it: The new film from the Oscar-winning team behind Free Solo; it premiered in Venice. The mountain footage hits differently on a big screen.

Reaction: TheWrap found it tragic and breathtaking; the RT score could not be confirmed yet.

Soul Patrol OPTIONAL

documentary · runtime and rating not confirmed

Nov 6 · limited release: IFC Center

Story: J.M. Harper's documentary tells the story of the first all-Black American special operations unit in the Vietnam War.

Why see it: Brings a little-known piece of history to light; it premiered at Sundance.

Reaction: RT 95%; The Playlist found it powerful but flawed, and some critics feel it misses the bigger picture.

Still playing, don't miss

Netflix · drama · 2 h 37 min · R · Spanish

ANGELIKA HOUSTON, 35mm · Wed–Thu 11:45 am, 3:05, 6:30, 10:00 pm · on Netflix Dec 2

You Can See Everything RECOMMENDED

A24 · documentary · 2 h 54 min · R

VILLAGE EAST · Wed 2:00, 3:15, 4:45, 8:30, 9:35 pm · Thu 6 shows, 1:00–10:00 pm

Story: Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim follow Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in her last month before prison.

Why see it: A formal experiment that blurs the line between documentary and performance; the less you know going in, the better. RT 96%; some critics find it too long.

Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother RECOMMENDED

Amazon MGM / Orion · action, thriller · 112 min · R

VILLAGE EAST · Wed 1:40, 4:20, 6:00 (captioned), 10:00 pm · Thu 2:00, 4:40, 7:20, 10:00 pm

Story: In Bassam Tariq's film, Mahershala Ali plays a devout hitman who returns to Houston for his wife's funeral; John Cho and Giancarlo Esposito co-star.

Why see it: Ali and Tariq reunite after their cancelled Blade project; it brings faith, grief and violence together inside a genre film. RT 96%, Metacritic 83.

The Only Living Pickpocket in New York OPTIONAL

crime, drama · 88 min · R

ANGELIKA HOUSTON · Wed 10:30 am, 12:45, 3:00, 5:15, 7:40 (captioned), 9:55 pm

Story: In Noah Segan's film, John Turturro plays a pickpocket nearing 70 who lands in trouble after stealing the wrong person's bag; Steve Buscemi and Giancarlo Esposito co-star.

Why see it: A Turturro performance counted among his best, with New York as the film's emotional center. Time Out 4/5; the side plots are considered weak.

Wicker OPTIONAL

fantasy romantic comedy · 105 min · R

ANGELIKA HOUSTON · Wed 10:45 am, 1:15 (captioned), 3:45, 6:20, 10:00 pm

Story: An outcast fisherwoman (Olivia Colman) has the village basket maker (Peter Dinklage) weave her a husband out of wicker; the husband who results (Alexander Skarsgård) turns the village upside down. Directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson.

Why see it: Colman and Skarsgård's chemistry and the handmade effects are widely praised. RT 94%; stretched from a short story, it sags in places.
