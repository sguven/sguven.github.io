@TheDayInBrief: The News in One Post

An X account that keeps a short running record of world events: every two to three days AI reads the news since the last post and sums it up in one witty, 280-character post.

01 · Problem

What need does it solve?

Most news is forgotten within a week. The idea was a small “note to history”: a timeline where each post says what actually happened in a few days, from politics and the economy to history-making events or a notable film, readable in seconds.

The aim was for the account to run on its own, from reading the news to publishing the post.

02 · Solution

How it works

A scheduled Claude task runs every two to three days. On every run it:

  • Researches the news since the last post across politics, the economy, science and culture
  • Picks the few events most likely to matter later, not only market moves
  • Writes one clever English summary that keeps the essential details: who, what and the key numbers
  • Checks the facts against its sources and keeps the post to a single tweet of 280 characters or less, with no threads
  • Schedules it through Typefully for the evening slot, with no approval step

A real post from the account:

Nov 6–9: Israel's president passed over Netanyahu and tapped Gadi Eisenkot, backed by 67 lawmakers, Arab-led parties included; he has 28 days to find 61 seats. COP31 opened in Antalya, agenda courtesy of Copernicus: 2026 is set to be the second-hottest year on record.

@TheDayInBrief · November 10, 2026

03 · Architecture

How the system fits together

One routine runs on a schedule. It researches the period since the previous post, writes the summary and checks it, then hands it to Typefully, which publishes it on X. Nothing waits for a manual approval.

05 · Challenges

Hard parts and how they were solved

  1. Several days in one post

    The publishing tool's free plan allows ten posts a month, so the account posts every two to three days and each post covers everything since the last one.

  2. A lot of news in 280 characters

    The post keeps names and numbers and drops filler, so it stays a single tweet without losing what matters.

  3. Choosing what matters

    The brief is a record for later, so it favours events with lasting weight over the day's noise.

  4. Accuracy with nobody watching

    Every fact and number in the post comes from the sources found in that run, and the length is checked before scheduling.

06 · Results

What came out of it

  • Live since November 6, 2026, posting on its own every two to three days.
  • Stays within the publishing tool's free plan.
  • A growing timeline of world events, each period summed up in one post.
